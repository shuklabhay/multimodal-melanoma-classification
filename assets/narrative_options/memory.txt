Fragmentary: Isolated details remembered, missing chronology and measurements; Ex: "Sometimes it itches after showers; I can’t recall when it started or if it’s bigger."
Uncertain vague: Fuzzy recall, no times or sizes, unsure of change, no anchors, no numbers; Ex: "I noticed it sometime last year, it might be a bit bigger, not sure."
Overconfident vague: Strong certainty with imprecise facts, confident tone without numbers; Ex: "It’s definitely fine and exactly normal-sized, I just know it’s the usual shape."
Contradictory soft: Minor conflicts within the account, hedged claims that don’t align perfectly; Ex: "It hasn’t really changed, though I guess it’s a bit larger than before."
Anchored but fuzzy: Tied to an event, numbers still vague or approximate; Ex: "Since the beach trip earlier this summer it seems a bit darker, maybe slightly larger."
Approximate: Broad windows and rough comparisons, mild uncertainty, few simple size words; Ex: "I first saw it around March, about pea-sized, maybe a touch darker."
Mildly precise: Clear duration ranges and concrete comparisons, no exact dates or measurements; Ex: "It changed over six to eight weeks, the edge looks uneven, it feels rough like sandpaper."
Precise: Exact dates and anchors; Ex: "I first noticed it on July 4, I first noticed it when it snagged on a towel and bled."
Episodic precise: Vivid scene tied to a specific event plus precise timing; Ex: "On a hike after my sister’s wedding in late August, left calf spot stung with sunscreen."
Short-interval precise: Clear but brief timing windows that are tighter than approximate, still without exact dates; Ex: "Across three to five days it shifted from flat to slightly raised, with the change happening noticeably each morning."
Chronologically detailed: Multi-step timeline with consistent ordering and durations, no exact dates; Ex: "It stayed flat for about a month, thickened over the next two weeks, then darkened slightly in the last week."
Highly specific comparative: Exact relative change described with a clear reference point, still no strict measurements; Ex: "It became noticeably sharper along the top edge after mid-September, compared to the smooth outline it had before."
